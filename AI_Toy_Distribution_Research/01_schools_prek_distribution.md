## 1. K-12 School District Purchasing Process

### 1.1 Decision-makers — formal authority vs. early influence

K-12 procurement is a **multi-stakeholder, layered** process. The labels below combine three independent sources that converge on the same structure.

**Formal approval authority (signs contracts, controls budget):**
- **Superintendent / Assistant Superintendent** — final approval on district-wide, multi-department purchases (curriculum, infrastructure, district-wide tech).
- **Procurement Director / Purchasing Department** — runs RFPs, negotiates contracts, enforces compliance.
- **School Board** — approves major expenditures and budgets; rarely the day-to-day contact.
- **Chief Financial Officer (CFO)** — gatekeeper for budget and ongoing-cost concerns.
- **IT Director / CTO / District Tech Coordinator** — evaluates integration, security, SSO, infrastructure. Can veto on technical grounds.

**Early "invisible season" influencers (shape the RFP before it is written):**
- **Curriculum Director / Content Specialist** — defines instructional alignment, framework requirements, PD expectations.
- **Director / Executive Director of Curriculum, Instruction, or Technology** — the "department head" who is often the primary buyer for a single-department product.
- **Title I Director** — controls federal Title I supplemental-instruction dollars ($18B nationally); relevant for products aimed at economically disadvantaged students.
- **Title IV / Federal Programs Director** — controls Title IV funds ($1.3B nationally) for "well-rounded education, student safety, effective technology use."
- **Principal** — the school-level buyer. "Your individual school-level buyer is almost always the school principal" (PRP Group). Selling one school can tip a district.
- **Teachers / Special Education Coordinators** — cannot purchase, but they drive pilots and recommendations. "When educators are involved from the beginning, they are much more likely to buy in" (EdTech Magazine, quoting IXL's Robin Munsell).

### 1.3 Procurement thresholds and the RFP process

- **Bid thresholds** vary by district, typically **$5,000–$50,000**. Below the threshold, schools can issue quotes or sole-source; above it, formal competitive bidding is required.
  - Source: https://www.nationgraph.com/post/education-technology-companies-k12-procurement
- **Vehicle types:** **RFP** (vendor proposes solution), **RFQ** (price-only on defined specs), **Sole Source** (only one viable vendor; requires written justification).
- **Cooperative contracts** (Sourcewell, OMNIA Partners, TIPS, E&I, NASPO ValuePoint) let districts piggyback on a single competitively-bid master contract. Holding one of these **significantly shortens** the per-district sales cycle. There are 50,000+ agencies on Sourcewell alone.
  - https://www.sourcewell-mn.gov/cooperative-purchasing
  - https://www.omniapartners.com/suppliers/really-good-stuff (RGS example)
  - https://www.tips-usa.com/assets/Vendorspdf/Classroom_&_Teaching_Aids_2111915_Kaplan_Early_Learning_Company_Contract.pdf (Kaplan TIPS contract)

### 1.4 Typical RFP / pilot structure

From the EdTech Magazine / IXL / SMART Technologies guidance:

- **Pilot duration:** **3–6 months** is typical; longer than a school year risks losing momentum. Some student-facing pilots run ~1 year (EdWeek, Oct 2025).
- **Pilot group composition:** Mix of engaged teachers, deliberate skeptics, and school leaders. "Giving an early voice to potential doubters may help surface concerns and build broader buy-in" (Robin Munsell, IXL).
- **Three measurement points:** baseline survey → midpoint survey → post-survey.
- **Standard success metrics districts look for:** usage metrics, progress against defined success measures, teacher interviews, classroom observations.
- **Seven steps to scale (SMART's Kris Astle):** (1) Review pilot findings vs. original success criteria, (2) Document lessons learned & implementation requirements, (3) Identify educator champions, (4) Scale PD alongside deployment, (5) Continue measuring adoption/outcomes, (6) [implied: align funding], (7) [implied: formal procurement].
- **District evaluation stages (NationGraph):** (1) Initial screening for licensing/insurance/data privacy compliance, (2) Instructional alignment review, (3) Technical evaluation (SSO, integration, security), (4) **30–90 day pilot**, (5) Reference checks / site visits.

Sources:
- https://edtechmagazine.com/k12/article/2026/08/how-run-successful-k-12-ed-tech-pilot-program-perfcon
- https://schoolai.com/blog/pilot-process-for-edtech-tools
- https://www.thetechedvocate.org/8-steps-to-successfully-piloting-an-edtech-project/

### 1.5 Funding sources the toy can be positioned against

- **Title I** ($18B/yr) — supplemental instruction for low-income students. Cannot replace core curriculum. Compatible if the AI toy is supplemental, tutoring-style.
- **Title IV, Part A** ($1.3B/yr) — "effective use of technology" — the cleanest fit for edtech hardware/software that supports instruction.
- **ESSER** — pandemic-era funds **expiring**; vendors must shift positioning to sustainable funding.
- **IDEA** — special education; relevant if the toy has SPED use cases.
- **E-Rate** — only for connectivity (Category 1) and some internal connections (Category 2). Generally **not** applicable to end-user AI devices.
- **State adoption funds** — in adoption states (TX, CA, FL) major instructional-material purchases happen on 5–7 year cycles; hardware like a smart toy is less bound by these cycles.

Sources:
- https://www.nationgraph.com/post/education-technology-companies-k12-procurement

## 3. COPPA vs. FERPA for School Deployment

Both apply to a smart toy collecting data from children under 13 in a school context. **They overlap and reinforce each other; neither replaces the other.**

### 3.1 Side-by-side

| | **COPPA** | **FERPA** |
|---|---|---|
| **Citation** | 15 U.S.C. §§ 6501–6506; **16 CFR Part 312** (FTC-enforced) | 20 U.S.C. § 1232g; **34 CFR Part 99** (Dept. of Education) |
| **Who it covers** | Operators of online services with **actual knowledge** of collecting data from children **under 13**, or services **directed to children under 13** | Recipients of federal education funding (schools, districts) and their "school officials" — which **includes edtech vendors under contract** |
| **Core trigger for vendors** | Any collection of personal info from a child under 13 | Receiving "education records" or "PII from education records" |
| **Consent model** | **Verifiable parental consent** required **before** collection (signed form, credit card, gov ID, knowledge-based) | **School** can consent on parents' behalf when disclosure is to a school official performing an institutional function — but only for the educational context |
| **Required notices** | Direct notice to parents of what is collected and how | Annual notification of rights; schools must log disclosures |
| **Data minimization** | Yes — collect only what's needed for the activity | Yes — limited to "legitimate educational interest" |
| **Enforcement** | FTC — civil penalties up to **$53,088 per violation** (2024 inflation-adjusted figure; verify current) per 16 CFR 1.98(d) | Dept. of Ed — loss of federal funding; private right of action via § 1983 |
| **AI-specific** | **2025 FTC amendments** tightened notice/consent/data-retention. School consent framework narrowed. | AI is treated as a "school official" function; output data is an education record if identifiable |
| **Key vendor requirement** | Age-gate; verifiable parental consent flow; COPPA-compliant privacy policy | Sign a Data Protection Agreement (DPA) designating vendor as a school official with § 99.33(a) controls; specify permitted uses; prohibit redisclosure |

Sources:
- https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions
- https://blog.promise.legal/startup-central/edtech-student-data-privacy-compliance/
- https://blog.promise.legal/ferpa-compliance-edtech-ai-checklist/
- https://studentprivacy.ed.gov/audience/education-technology-vendors
- https://www.hireplicity.com/blog/ferpa-compliance-checklist-2025
- https://www.appitsoftware.com/blog/ferpa-ai-privacy-educational-systems

### 3.2 What schools/districts require from an AI vendor in practice

- **Signed Data Protection Agreement (DPA)** addressing FERPA § 99.33(a) — required before purchase in most districts.
- **Student Data Privacy Consortium (SDPC) National Data Privacy Agreement** framework, or a state-specific version — many districts will not start vendor conversations without it.
- **Student Privacy Pledge** signatory — voluntary but signals baseline compliance.
- **Encryption in transit & at rest, RBAC, audit logs, incident response plan** — "reasonable security procedures" standard.
- **Data flow documentation:** every field collected, every subprocessor, every cloud region.
- **AI-specific disclosure:** NYC requires vendors to disclose any AI technologies processing school data via OneTrust assessments; the trend is spreading.
- **Age-gating / parental consent flow** if any user could be under 13.
- **Granular data deletion capability** — delete on request from school, on contract termination, by student/school/district.
- **No targeted advertising, no profiling for non-educational purposes, no selling of student data.**
- **Aggregate analytics** are acceptable only with minimum-cell anonymization (commonly cited as 10 students per cell).

Sources:
- https://www.magicschool.ai/  / https://schoolai.com/ (vendor-side data privacy pages)
- https://careerclutch.ai/blog/ny-ed-law-2-d-compliant-education-vendor-guide.html
- https://www.cybernut.com/blog/all-about-sopipa-californias-law-protecting-student-data-in-the-digital-age
- https://www.briskteaching.com/fr-fr/post/answering-ai-questions-for-district-leaders (Brisk Teaching lists FERPA/COPPA, signed DPAs, Clever/Canvas/PowerSchool compatibility as procurement norms)

### 3.3 State-level requirements (beyond NY and CA)

Per the **Public Interest Privacy Center**, ~150 student privacy laws have been passed across **47 states + DC**. The most-cited vendor-facing laws:

| State law | Citation | What it requires of vendors |
|---|---|---|
| **California SOPIPA** | Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code **§ 22584** | Prohibits targeted advertising, profiling, selling student data; mandates "reasonable security"; data deletion on request; **>20 states have modeled laws on SOPIPA** |
| **California AB 1584** (Cal. Ed. Code § 49073.1) | Companion to SOPIPA | School must contractually bind vendors to SOPIPA terms |
| **New York Education Law § 2-d** + 8 NYCRR Part 121 | NYSED | Vendors receiving PII must sign a Data Protection Agreement with NY-mandated terms; NYSED Chief Privacy Officer enforces; schools must post a Parents' Bill of Rights; **every NY educational agency must designate a Data Protection Officer**; AI disclosures required |
| **Colorado** | SB 18-058 / SOPIPA-style | Vendor obligations similar to SOPIPA |
| **Connecticut** | CT SB 949, PA 16-189 | Prohibits targeted advertising, data selling |
| **Illinois SOPPA** | 105 ILCS 85 | Amends SOPPA for IL; expanded data categories |
| **Texas** | SB 1397 (2019) | District must post a data governance policy; vendor DPA required for many ops |

**Operational impact for a Pre-K AI smart toy sold nationally:** the vendor must be ready to sign a state-specific DPA in at least **NY, CA, IL, CO, CT, TX**, and adopt SDPC NDPA for the rest.

### 4.7 Recommended go-to-market order

1. **Get onto a cooperative contract** (TIPS, OMNIA Partners, Sourcewell, Purchasing Connection). Required documents: W-9, COI (commercial general liability + product liability typically **$1M–$2M** per occurrence is standard for school vendors), COPPA/FERPA DPA template, SOS business registration, SAM.gov UEI. Channel: bids@kaplanco.com (Kaplan) and the equivalent bid desks at RGS / School Specialty.
2. **Direct district pilot** at 1–3 Title I schools (Pre-K or K) using the 30–90-day pilot framework.
3. **Join the SDPC NDPA** so the toy can be added to district-approved vendor lists with one signature.
4. **Apply to AdoptAClassroom.org** as a vendor partner — teacher-direct channel, low friction. https://www.adoptaclassroom.org/vendor-partners/
5. **Educator-direct** via Lakeshore Rewards or Kaplan MyKaplan with a 15% educator discount as the headline offer.
6. **E-Rate is generally not applicable** to end-user AI devices.

## 5. Key Risk and Gap Flags

- **No public MOQ/payment terms** for 4 of 5 distributors — must be obtained by direct contact with each sales rep.
- **Pilot cycle is the gating step:** 3–6 months typical; do not plan revenue before pilot end + board approval.
- **COPPA 2025 amendments** tightened parental-consent mechanics; verify current FTC guidance before product launch.
- **NY Ed Law 2-d and CA SOPIPA are de facto national standards** because NY and CA districts are large and many other states have modeled laws on SOPIPA. Plan for compliance in both from day one.
- **The $149–$349 price point for a Pre-K AI smart toy is currently unserved at scale** — Khan Academy Kids / ABCmouse / HOMER / LeapFrog Academy are all software apps, not hardware companions. Miko 3 is the closest physical competitor but is home-positioned.
