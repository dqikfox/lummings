### 1.2 Distribution channels

- **Direct from manufacturer** — Tobii Dynavox and PRC-Saltillo both sell through regional consultant networks (a regional SLP/AAC specialist does the eval, recommends the device, and supports funding paperwork).
- **DME (Durable Medical Equipment) dealers** — for SGDs paid by Medicaid/Medicare/commercial insurance. Tobii Dynavox and PRC-Saltillo operate manufacturer-direct billing arms; devices can also be fulfilled via general DME suppliers (e.g., Numotion, Apria, Liberator Medical) once funding is secured.
- **Specialty AAC vendors** — Logantech, Saltillo and Tobii have direct e-commerce for retail/uninsured buyers.

### 1.3 What SLPs actually look for

Per the CMS SGD Local Coverage Determination (LCD L33739), an SLP must produce a **formal written evaluation** that includes:
1. Current communication impairment (type, severity, course, language & cognitive ability)
2. Whether other natural modes of communication could meet daily needs
3. Functional communication goals & treatment options considered
4. **Rationale for selection of a specific device and any accessories**
5. Training schedule for the device
6. Cognitive and physical abilities to use the device and accessories
7. For upgrades, functional benefit vs. initial device

Source: CMS SGD LCD L33739 — https://www.cms.gov/medicare-coverage-database/view/lcd.aspx?LCDId=33739

Decision criteria that recur in SLP practice (cross-referenced from Rehabmart reviews and AAC clinical sources):
- Voice quality and personalization (Acapela voices, voice-banking)
- Language-system fit (LAMP, Unity, PODD, TD Snap, Core Word)
- Access method (touch, keyguard, switch, eye gaze, head tracking)
- Durability, mounting, portability, battery life
- Symbol clarity and caregiver editing tools
- Funding-track record (CMS-approved codes, prior-auth patterns)

> **Implication for the AI toy:** It cannot displace a dedicated SGD (CMS coverage rules require *severe expressive speech impairment*, not "any communication support"). It can plausibly sit **upstream** of an SGD as a language-stimulation / modeling tool, or as a low-cost companion for kids who would never qualify for a $5K device (e.g., pragmatic-language, social-reciprocity, early intervention).

### 2.3 Who decides on tools

The buy-side for clinical tools in ABA is layered:
- **Owner/operator** — budget authority; signs off on any non-consumable spend; for small practices this is the same person as clinical director.
- **Clinical director / Regional clinical director** — evaluates clinical effectiveness, fit to treatment protocols, data-collection value; gates "does this improve outcomes?"
- **BCBA** — end user; specifies whether a tool produces reliable, generalizable, measurable data (the requirement for BCBA supervision per BACB ethics code, cited by Neuromnia, Alpaca Health).
- **RBT (Registered Behavior Technician)** — line-level data entry; usability matters.
- **VP of Clinical Services / "Clinical Excellence Leaders"** at larger orgs (e.g., Centria job listing for Director of ABA Clinical Services) — set org-wide tool standards.

Sources: https://applyall.com/jobs/us/director-of-aba-clinical-services-at-centriaautism-jc_e1e6a00965b928ca682a3038 ; https://behavioristbookclub.com/implementing-new-tech-in-an-aba-clinic-adoption-training-and-change-management-tools-templates-and-checklists/ ; https://www.alpacahealth.io/provider-resources/ai-automation-healthcare-bcba

### 2.4 How new tools are evaluated

Themes from the BCBA/clinical-tech literature:
1. **Outcome data — does it produce measurable behavior change?** Tech without measurable effect is a hard sell.
2. **Data quality / audit trail** — must satisfy BACB documentation & payor audit requirements.
3. **HIPAA + state privacy compliance** — explicit requirement that AI/LLM use be disclosed and segmented (Behavior Academy: "outputs are always reviewed by a qualified professional"). https://www.thebehavioracademy.com/blog/llm-wrappers-in-aba
4. **EHR/PM integration** — major pain point; a tool that doesn't plug into CentralReach, Catalyst, Rethink, AccuPoint, Hi Rasmus, etc. adds friction. https://medium.com/@s-cubed/choosing-the-right-aba-practice-management-solution-for-large-vs-small-clinics-8976574a3470
5. **Pilot-first rollout** — typical pattern: single clinic 4–8 week pilot, gather BCBA/RBT feedback, then committee decision.
6. **Risk-tiering** — anything that touches clinical decision-making is held to a higher bar than back-office or scheduling tools.

### 2.5 Tech/screen-based tools in ABA sessions today

- Discrete Trial Training (DTT) — the iconic 1:1 table-top, often pictured with **tablets and timers**, is "what many people picture when they think of ABA therapy" — https://abacarenetwork.com/blog/discrete-trial-training
- Common categories of in-session tech: data-collection apps (ABA ToolSet, Catalyst, DataFinch), visual schedule apps, video modeling, token-economy apps, AAC apps (PECS phase apps, TouchChat), and increasingly VR (Floreo received FDA Breakthrough Device designation) — https://apex-social.com/autism-tech-breakthroughs-2024/
- A 2024 systematic review (33 RCTs, 1,285 participants) found digital health interventions were associated with **greater improvements in core ASD symptoms or IQ** vs. control conditions — cited by Ouva's 2026 ABA apps guide, https://ouva.co/blog/top-autism-aba-therapy-apps-digital-tools-2026/
- **Caveat:** I could not locate a published % figure for "what % of ABA sessions currently involve screens/tablets." Industry sources describe pervasive tablet use during DTT/centers but no hard statistic was found; this is a defensible assumption based on operational descriptions but **not** a citable number. Flagging as a gap to validate.

**Critical rule for our toy:** CPT 97153 (RBT-delivered direct therapy) is the main per-session labor billable code. It is **time-based and person-delivered**, so the toy's role can only be **embedded into the session** — it does **not** generate a separate reimbursement line for the practice. This sharply limits how much a clinic will pay per unit (they'll pay out of session margin, not against a billable).

## 3. Special-Needs Funding & Medicaid Waivers

### 3.1 HCBS waivers (1915(c)) are the primary funding channel

- Home & Community-Based Services waivers are run by **every state + DC**; a state can operate multiple waivers (Autism, Intellectual/Developmental Disabilities, Aging, etc.).
- Source: Autism Speaks — https://www.autismspeaks.org/blog/home-and-community-based-services-hcbs-waivers
- Federal CMS authority — https://www.medicaid.gov/medicaid/home-community-based-services/home-community-based-services-authorities/home-community-based-services-1915c

### 3.2 Medicaid HCPCS codes for AAC / speech generating devices

| **E2500** | SGD, digitized speech, ≤8 min recording time |
| **E2502** | SGD, digitized speech, >8 min but ≤20 min |

| **E2508** | SGD, synthesized speech, requiring message formulation by spelling and access by direct selection |

### 3.3 Critical CMS rule for our toy

> "Desktop computers, laptop computers, tablets, smartphones, and other hand-held computers (general computing devices) aren't considered DME because they don't meet requirements 3, 4, 6, and 7, even though they may serve a medical purpose. We reimburse for speech generating software only (HCPCS code E2511) when it's installed on a general computing device." — CMS, https://www.cms.gov/training-education/medicare-learning-networkr-mln/compliance/medicare-provider-compliance-tips/speech-generating-devices

**Implication:** A general-purpose AI toy that *also* plays videos and games **cannot qualify as DME under E2510** — it fails "primarily used to serve a medical purpose" and "not useful to a person without illness or injury." This rules out SGD-style Medicaid funding. The realistic path is HCBS waiver funding (3.4) for "assistive technology" line items — which are reviewed case-by-case and rarely cover consumer-electronic AI toys.

### 3.4 HCBS AT coverage is widespread but case-by-case

- AT3 Center state-by-state waiver directory: https://at3center.net/state-medicaid-waivers/
- A 2026 MACPAC scan covered **263 state HCBS waivers that included at least one AT category** — https://openminds.com/market-intelligence/resources/042026statemedicaidatinhcbs/
- Approximately **47 of 51 U.S. jurisdictions** have a Medicaid waiver with a verified equipment-funding category accessible to children — Brainrichkids state-by-state guide, https://brainrichkids.com/blogs/decision-guide-how-to-choose-your-home-play-gym/state-disability-waiver-programs-sensory-equipment

### 3.5 How providers get on state-approved vendor lists

Steps distilled from state provider-enrollment docs:
1. **Submit Medicaid provider enrollment application** for the specific service category (e.g., "Assistive Technology Services," T2028 code, etc.)
2. Provide policies & procedures, licensure/certifications, EIN, NPI, insurance certificates, business formation docs.
3. Background checks, fingerprinting in many states.
4. Pass state review; receive a Medicaid provider ID; bill via that ID.

Example sources:
- Nevada: https://www.waivergroup.com/post/assistive-technology-services-provider-in-nevada
- DC DDS: https://dds.dc.gov/page/hcbs-waiver-provider-application-process
- Utah New Choices Waiver: https://medicaid-documents.dhhs.utah.gov/Documents/pdfs/ltc/nc/Assistive+Technology+Devices+T2028+-+Att+B.pdf
- General guide: https://www.aveecare.com/resources/medicaid-waiver-programs-guide

**Realistic timeline:** 3–9 months state-by-state. Per-state enrollment makes national Medicaid coverage a heavy lift for a small company.

### 3.6 Other grant/funding channels (non-Medicaid)

- State autism-specific waivers (e.g., CA, FL, MA, NY, PA, TX — vary in eligibility and benefit limits).
- School-district assistive-tech budgets (IEP/504-driven).
- Private foundations: Autism Speaks, Doug Flutie Jr. Foundation, ACT Today!, United Healthcare Children's Foundation, Variety, Dream Foundation.
- Tricare/Echo for military dependents.
- ABLE accounts (tax-advantaged savings for disability expenses).

> **Bottom line on funding path:** Treat Medicaid SGD coverage as **not viable** for a general-purpose AI companion. Treat HCBS waiver AT coverage as **possible but case-by-case and slow**. Plan the go-to-market assuming families pay out of pocket + reimbursement via FSAs/HSAs/ABLE, with insurance-funded acquisition as a long-term multi-year channel.

## 4. Pediatric Hospital Distribution — Child Life Specialists

### 4.1 Who they are

- **Certified Child Life Specialist (CCLS)** — credential issued by the **Association of Child Life Professionals (ACLP)**.
- ACLP history: formed in 1982 (as the Child Life Council); ~5,000 members from ~600 organizations (2012 membership figure; current membership larger). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_Child_Life_Professionals
- Certification path: bachelor's degree (often in child life, child development, psychology), **600-hour internship**, passing score on the certification exam. Some employers expect a master's. https://www.childlife.org/Certification ; https://www.humanservicesedu.org/child-life-specialist/
- Compensation (per Salary.com aggregate): ~$55K–$65K for 10–14 yrs experience; BLS broad-category reference ~$58K. https://online.edgewood.edu/blog/child-life-specialist-salary/ ; https://www.humanservicesedu.org/child-life-specialist/

### 4.2 What they do

Per ACLP and Wikipedia: Child life specialists help children and families cope with hospitalization, illness, and disability through:
- Preparation for procedures (medical play, dolls, picture books)
- Procedural support and distraction during painful/scary events
- Therapeutic medical play
- Non-pharmacological pain management
- Sibling and bereavement support

### 4.3 How they evaluate products

There is **no single formal ACLP "approved product list"** for hospital-wide procurement. In practice:
1. **Donations and trials** are coordinated through hospital Foundations, Volunteer Services, or the Child Life department directly.
2. Products must pass **hospital infection-control** (cleanable surfaces, no small parts that can't be disinfected, ideally no fabric that harbors pathogens).
3. Products must pass **safety/CPSC** review for age grading; the toy must be appropriate for the patient population (oncology, NICU, general peds, behavioral health, ER — different rules).
4. **CLS informal review** — clinical utility, developmental fit, ease of cleaning, durability, cost per patient-use.
5. Hospital Procurement and Value Analysis committees for any purchase > trivial dollar amount.

### 4.4 The "gift shop" channel

Most children's hospitals operate **gift shops** as auxiliary fundraisers (e.g., Lucile Packard Children's Hospital Stanford gift shop is run by the "Roth Auxiliary" — https://www.stanfordchildrens.org/en/patients-families/gift-shop.html). These are typically:
- Operated by hospital auxiliary volunteers, not merchandising professionals.
- Stock mostly convenience items, baby items, plush toys, balloons.
- Have thin selection criteria — but not CPSC-bypassing. Same consumer-product safety law applies.
- Wholesale-on-hand, consignment, or donated-stock models.

### 4.5 Real entry paths for our AI toy

1. **Charitable donation pipeline** — work with established hospital-toy charities:
   - **Child's Play Charity** (gaming-origin, funds toys & games to 170+ hospitals worldwide) — https://childsplaycharity.org/news/post/association-of-child-life-professionals-conference
   - **Project Sunshine**, **Kids in Need of Defense**, etc.
2. **Direct child life program outreach** — pitch to the CCLS department at children's hospitals (typically ~250+ pediatric hospitals in the US). They trial and can recommend integration into "distraction kits," procedure-prep kits, sensory rooms.
3. **Industry conference presence** — **ACLP Annual Conference** is the canonical venue; product demos and trial distribution.
4. **Hospital gift shop wholesale** — wholesale margin expected (typically 50–60% retail), with sales-volume risk low. Best as a slow brand-build not as a primary channel.
5. **Specialty pediatric distributors** — pediatric specialty distributors carry selected therapeutic toys; these require sales-rep relationships, EDI, compliance docs.

> **Caveat:** I could not find a public source quantifying "average margin required for a product to clear hospital value-analysis committee" or "average 6–12 mo sales cycle into a children's hospital system." This is a defensible assumption (matches general medical-device onboarding norms) but **not** citable. Flagging.

- **Evaluation period:** 30–60 day classroom pilot is common for new tools.

### 5.3 Direct-to-classroom-pack pricing for our toy (recommended starting point)

Translating the precedent set by Lexia/DreamBox into hardware pricing:
- **Classroom pack (5–30 units)** — anchored to retail $149–$349 → standard educator discount **15–25% off list** (so ~$112–$262 per device). Many edtech vendors give a **free device** at thresholds (e.g., 1 free per 20).
- **School-wide pack (30+ units)** — flat $5K–$12K per school band, or **20–30% off list** plus free accessories/subscriptions.
- **District master agreement** — multi-year commit; expected **30–40% off** plus professional-development and onboarding package.

These numbers are extrapolations from edtech licensing norms; verify against specific district procurement officers before quoting.

### 5.4 Decision makers for school sales

- **Teacher** — requestor; rarely the budget-holder.
- **Principal** — site budget authority for small purchases.
- **District Curriculum Director / Director of Special Education** — programmatic fit.
- **Director of Technology / CTO** — device management, network, MDM.
- **Purchasing / Procurement** — PO process; vendor onboarding.
- **Special Education Director** — **the single most relevant buyer for an autism-support / AAC-adjacent / speech-support AI toy in schools.** Decision criteria: alignment with IEP goals, evidence base, MTSS tiers, RTI/MTSS integration.

> **Hardest gate to clear in K-12 sales:** getting on a state-approved instructional materials list and/or district-approved vendor list. State adoption lists (e.g., TX SBOE, CA, FL, NY) confer massive distribution leverage but require an evidence-base review that can take 12–24 months.

## Cross-Cutting Distribution Implications for the $149–$349 AI Toy

1. **Reimbursement strategy ≠ sales strategy for core product.** The toy cannot qualify as DME/SGD, so do **not** anchor pricing to Medicaid-funded AAC device norms.
2. **Best-funded "niche" entry point:** HCBS waiver AT lines (case-by-case), school special-ed budgets (IEP-driven), Tricare ECHO supplements — all slow, multi-year channels.
