## 1. Insurance Reimbursement for Assistive Communication Devices

### 1.1 HCPCS codes that matter (verified from CMS)

The relevant HCPCS codes for Speech Generating Devices (SGDs) and accessories:

- **E2500** – Speech-generating device, digitized speech, using pre-recorded messages, ≤8 minutes
- **E2502** – SGD, digitized speech, using pre-recorded messages, >8 minutes but ≤20 minutes
- **E2504** – SGD, digitized speech, using pre-recorded messages, >20 minutes but ≤40 minutes
- **E2506** – SGD, digitized speech, using pre-recorded messages, >40 minutes
- **E2508** – SGD, synthesized speech, requiring message formulation by spelling
- **E2510** – SGD, synthesized speech, permitting multiple methods of message formulation and multiple methods of device access (most commonly used)
- **E2511** – SGD, software, for personal computing device, tablet (NOT separately payable under Medicare DME in most cases — see §1.3)
- **E2512** – Accessory for speech-generating device, mounting system
- **E2599** – Accessory for speech-generating device, NOC (not otherwise classified)

Source: CMS Article A52469 (SGD Policy Article) — https://www.cms.gov/medicare-coverage-database/view/article.aspx?articleId=52469
Source: ASHA Leader Bottom Line — https://leader.pubs.asha.org/doi/10.1044/leader.BML.18092013.28

### 1.2 The "dedicated device" rule (critical)

Per CMS Article A52469: a device that "provides the same functionality as a desktop, tablet, smartphone or laptop computer at the time of issuance is considered a general use computer and is **not** considered a speech generating device." General computing devices with non-SGD features included at initial issue must be coded **A9270** (non-covered).

PDAC verification required: since June 1, 2016, only products on the PDAC Product Classification List (DMECS) may be billed to Medicare using E2510.

Sources:
- https://www.cms.gov/medicare-coverage-database/view/article.aspx?articleId=52469
- https://med.noridianmedicare.com/web/jddme/policies/dmd-articles/coverage-reminder-speech-generating-devices-revised
- https://medicareadvocacy.org/welcome-reprieve-for-people-who-need-speech-generating-devices-sgds-to-communicate/

### 1.3 What this means for the AI toy

**The AI toy almost certainly does NOT qualify as a Medicare/Medicaid-covered SGD.** It is a general-purpose consumer electronics product. To qualify, it would need:
1. Be on the PDAC Product Classification List (DMECS).
2. Be "dedicated" (locked to speech-generation only — which the toy is not, since it does general tutoring/companionship).
3. Have a written order from a physician and an SLP evaluation (CMS LCD L33739).
4. Pass the "primarily medical purpose" test — fails for an educational companion.

**Implication:** Don't position the toy as a covered DME. Pursue private insurance gaps and family grants instead.

### 1.4 Coverage when the device IS covered (for reference)

When SGDs qualify under Medicare Part B:
- Covered under DME benefit, 80% Medicare / 20% patient (after deductible).
- Rental capped at 13 months; ownership transfers after month 13.
- Coverage requires face-to-face physician visit within 6 months, detailed SLP written evaluation (LCD L33739), and PDAC code verification.

States run their own Medicaid programs with separate coverage rules and codes (often the same HCPCS). Many state Medicaid programs cover E2510 and accessories via DME benefit; fee schedules vary by state.

## 2. Family-Facing Grant Programs for Special-Needs Funding

### 2.1 United Healthcare Children's Foundation (UHCCF) — primary lever

- **Award:** up to **$5,000 per 12-month period**, **$10,000 lifetime maximum per child**
- **Eligibility:** child must be **16 or younger** at application; family must have commercial health insurance (any carrier — not just UnitedHealthcare); expenses must not be fully covered by insurance
- **Use:** "medical services/items" — assistive tech, therapy, equipment
- **Source:** https://www.uhccf.org/apply-for-a-grant/ and https://www.unitedhealthgroup.com/newsroom/2021/2021-2-24-uhccf-childrens-foundation-grant.html
- **Channel tactic:** Make the AI toy a reimbursable expense category. Maintain a "How to submit for UHCCF" one-pager on the site.

### 2.3 IDEA Part B (school-age, ages 3–21) and Part C (birth–3)

- IDEA requires schools to provide AT devices and services needed for a student to benefit from special education.
- Funding comes from the district; AT device requests go through the **IEP** (Individualized Education Program) team.
- Each district receives an IDEA Part B allocation; per-child funding varies.
- Part C covers early intervention (birth–3) via state agency; AT may be funded through service plan.

Sources:
- https://www.wati.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/10/Ch16-FundingAT.pdf
- https://www.lowvisionsupply.com/blogs/resources/funding-at-students
- https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1184079.pdf

**Channel tactic:** Target AT specialists / special-education directors; pitch as "IEP-supportable" AT.

### 3.4 Competitive positioning takeaways

1. **The $149–$349 AI companion slot is wide open.** Moxie's exit removes the closest competitor; Miko 3 sits at the top of the band; Curio is the lower-end entrant.
2. **Miko 3 owns "AI companion" — to differentiate, lean adaptive/special-needs, IEP-fundable, classroom pack.**
3. **In the classroom AI-tutor slot, Khanmigo is free for teachers** — you must sell *hardware tangibility* and *engagement*, not tutoring capability alone.
4. **Subscription-led pricing** (like Miko Max) is the industry norm. Plan for a $5–10/mo recurring layer on top of $149–$349 hardware.

## 5. Strategic Takeaways for the AI Toy ($149–$349)

### 5.1 Funding-channel priority

1. **Direct-to-parent (DTC) on Amazon + own site** — primary; family UHCCF grants + Variety chapter advocacy.
2. **Special-education / IEP funding (IDEA Part B)** — district purchase via AT specialist; sells at the class-set price point.
3. **HCBS waiver AT funds** — slow, case-by-case, not a revenue lever.
4. **Tricare ECHO** — military families with special-needs dependents; coverage is real for AT.
5. **Do NOT pursue Medicare/Medicaid DME coverage** — the toy fails the "dedicated device" rule.

### 5.2 Pricing / packaging to enable funded channels

- A **$149 SKU** is well within UHCCF's typical grant size ($5K lifetime, $10K max). A "classroom pack of 5" at $699 would consume ~14% of one UHCCF award — pitchable.
- A **5-pack or 30-pack tier** is what districts want (Lexia-style: per-seat pricing ~$20–$50; classroom/site licenses $5K–$12K). Model: per-seat $25–$40, with a site license floor of $4,000 (covers 30 devices + admin dashboard).
- **Subscription layer** ($5–10/mo per device or $99/yr family) — aligns with Miko Max ($99/yr) and CoughDrop ($200/yr).
