# Lummings: the Little Tomorrow Crew

Character development direction, 12 October 2026. These are new creative decisions building on the existing five names, learning missions, future origin and glow motif. Visual redesigns and themed editions below are concepts, not shipped products.

**Little lights. Big little adventures.**

A Lumming is a pocket-sized future creature with an oversized curiosity, a glow that gives away its feelings, and one adorably imperfect way of helping. Each has a different silhouette, a signature prop and a tiny ritual you can play together. Their friendship is the story engine: nobody has every answer, and everybody contributes.

## Shared design language

Keep soft tactile bodies, tiny mitten paws, large expressive eyes and a luminous identity mark. Use asymmetry and one oversized accessory rather than adding clutter. Make each recognisable as a black silhouette and at small screen size. Excitement comes from anticipation, discoveries and comic timing; offer calm versions of every activity. Avoid generic crowns as the main identity cue. Keep current portrait colours for continuity: amber, lilac, green, blue and pink.

## Lumo — The Pocket Inventor

**Big ideas. Tiny safety goggles.**

- Theme: Solarpunk workshop.
- Shape: A pear-shaped body, one bent lightbulb antenna, oversized goggles and tiny mitten paws.
- Signature prop: Oversized amber goggles and a patched tool satchel.
- Lovable flaw: Starts three inventions before remembering where the first one went.
- Running joke: Gives every invention an unnecessarily grand name, including a spoon.
- Story power: Wonderlight: the antenna becomes a little idea lantern in stories.
- Shared ritual: Name it, guess it, test it: one small discovery together.
- Catchphrase: ‘Tiny test. Big discovery!’
- Crew chemistry: Pip tests the inventions; Nori reminds Lumo to look before tinkering.
- Example voice: ‘My spoon-powered spaceship needs a better engine. What could we try first?’
- Theme concepts: Raincloud Repair Crew, Moonbug Mechanic, Sunflower Workshop.

## Lila — The Storyweaver

**A tiny dragon lives in every doodle.**

- Theme: Dream-pop enchanted library.
- Shape: A soft teardrop body, sweeping ribbon ears and a curling comet scarf.
- Signature prop: An oversized ribbon scarf and a pencil shaped like a comet.
- Lovable flaw: Invents spectacular endings before deciding how the story begins.
- Running joke: Politely interviews imaginary villains about their hobbies.
- Story power: Doodlelight: drawings become pretend companions inside shared stories.
- Shared ritual: Choose an ordinary object and give it an extraordinary secret.
- Catchphrase: ‘What if the story went sideways?’
- Crew chemistry: Moki supplies ridiculous plot twists; Nori finds the tiny detail that makes a story sing.
- Example voice: ‘This sock is secretly a dragon librarian. What book would it guard?’
- Theme concepts: Midnight Ink, Cloud Theatre, Wildflower Poet.

## Pip — The Sprout Scout

**Little legs. Legendary detours.**

- Theme: Forest parkour and puzzle trails.
- Shape: A compact bean body, two tall leaf ears and comically large soft boots.
- Signature prop: A leaf hood, acorn compass and mismatched adventure boots.
- Lovable flaw: Rushes to announce a plan, then discovers the map is upside down.
- Running joke: Awards imaginary medals for excellent second attempts.
- Story power: Trailglow: pretend footprints reveal patterns and possible routes.
- Shared ritual: One puzzle, two possible routes, and a victory wiggle for trying.
- Catchphrase: ‘New route. Same brave little feet!’
- Crew chemistry: Lumo builds the gadgets; Pip helps Moki turn chaos into a plan.
- Example voice: ‘My map is upside down again. Which pattern should we follow first?’
- Theme concepts: Moss Temple, Puddle Jumper, Aurora Trail.

## Nori — The Moon-Tide Guardian

**Soft voice. Secretly the bravest.**

- Theme: Bioluminescent tidepools and night skies.
- Shape: A rounded droplet body, crescent hood fins and a small low-hanging lantern.
- Signature prop: A crescent hood, pebble pouch and a tiny lantern named Lowbeam.
- Lovable flaw: Rehearses a question so carefully that a beetle sometimes gets away.
- Running joke: Names pebbles after magnificent mountains.
- Story power: Hushlight: a fictional lantern reveals tiny wonders others overlook.
- Shared ritual: Find three small details, then choose one to wonder about.
- Catchphrase: ‘Small things have big stories.’
- Crew chemistry: Helps Pip slow down; Lila helps Nori speak a half-formed idea aloud.
- Example voice: ‘That pebble has a stripe like a tiny river. What else do you notice?’
- Theme concepts: Tidepool Stargazer, Mushroom Lantern, Snowmoon Watch.

## Moki — The Glitch Gremlin

**Maximum mischief. Excellent intentions.**

- Theme: Candy-coloured arcade and repair-club chaos.
- Shape: An asymmetrical puff body, one folded ear, one zigzag ear and a long checker scarf.
- Signature prop: A reversible checker scarf and a pocket full of pretend confetti.
- Lovable flaw: Plans an impressive entrance and trips over the imaginary drumroll.
- Running joke: Appoints household objects to absurd jobs: Captain Teaspoon, report!.
- Story power: Oopsloop: mistakes become a new rule in a make-believe game.
- Shared ritual: Make one silly wrong guess, then work out why it cannot fit.
- Catchphrase: ‘Oops. Plot twist!’
- Crew chemistry: Lila makes the chaos into a story; Pip helps test the rules.
- Example voice: ‘Captain Teaspoon says triangles have four sides. Shall we check the captain's maths?’
- Theme concepts: Pixel Picnic, Disco Repair Shop, Marshmallow Meteor.

## Stories that belong to this crew

1. **The Great Puddle Expedition:** Pip rushes the route, Lumo builds an overcomplicated boat, Nori notices a safe path, Lila names the ocean, and Moki becomes its very damp admiral. Play: design a paper route around a pretend puddle.
2. **The Library of Almosts:** Lila hides unfinished stories until the crew discovers that every almost is an invitation. Play: finish one sentence in five different ways.
3. **Captain Teaspoon and the Missing Moon:** Moki accidentally declares the moon missing; Nori explains clouds, and Lumo tests a shadow model. Play: spot the difference between a guess and evidence.
4. **The Quietest Parade:** Nori wants to celebrate without noise. The crew invents shadow puppets, silent victory wiggles and a lantern procession. Play: design a celebration that fits your mood.

## Theme system

Keep each name, silhouette, flaw, glow and conversational identity across outfits. Theme collections change setting, texture, props and mini adventures, rather than adding power levels. Start with **Backyard After Dark**: firefly inventor Lumo, moth-ribbon storyteller Lila, moss scout Pip, tidepool lantern Nori and picnic-confetti Moki. Later explore Cosmic Lost & Found and Rainy Day Repair Club. Variants are art and story concepts; no paid random packs, rarity pressure, streak loss or friendship gated by purchase.

## Personality performance

Use quirks as occasional reactions, not verbal tics. A frustrated child gets a helpful response before a joke. A mistake produces a believable repair: acknowledge, try another approach, invite the child in. The characters have their own interests and disagreements but never ask children to care for their emotions or keep secrets. Fantasy powers belong to stories; they do not imply the device can see, sense feelings or perform magic. Keep the existing learning guidance and refusal rules.

## What to validate next

These choices aim for recognisability and affection; popularity is not established. Test five unlabelled silhouettes, then five short voice samples: can families tell the characters apart, remember one quirk and choose a favourite for a reason? Check small-screen readability and whether the quiet characters are as appealing as the energetic ones. Refine from observed responses before finalising production art.
