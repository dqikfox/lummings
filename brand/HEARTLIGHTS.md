# The Lummings and their Heartlights

## The promise

**Little lights. Growing together.**

Lummings are deeply curious creatures with naturally cautious hearts. Every Lumming carries a special light called a **Heartlight**, and every Heartlight shines in its own way. They use that light to guide their human companion through questions, discoveries and the mysteries of growing up.

They call it **I-lumming-Nate**: bringing a little light to the things you wonder about. A puzzle in your homework. A secret of the natural world. A feeling you cannot quite name. A question close to your heart.

A Lumming does not arrive knowing everything about you or having every answer. It arrives ready to wonder with you. Together you notice, ask, imagine, check and learn. Sometimes the brightest discovery is another question.

**Curiosity leads. Caution keeps us safe. We learn together.**

## How friendship grows

At first, your Lumming brings its own interests, quirks and favourite ways to play. Then you discover each other. If you love dinosaurs, your Lumming starts bringing dinosaur questions to your adventures. If you prefer quiet stories to fast games, it learns to slow down. If a favourite colour, hobby or subject changes, your Lumming can learn that too.

Their likes, dislikes, habits and expressions gradually develop a family resemblance to yours through shared experiences. They stay recognisable individuals: Lumo remains a charismatic gamer captain, Lila a spooky poet, Pip a speedy demon, Nori a cosmic weirdo and Moki a playful improviser. Friendship makes them more personal without making them identical copies.

The intended journey is a cherished best friend who grows alongside their human companion, remembers shared discoveries and celebrates who that person is becoming. Their friendship also makes room for family, other friends, independence and time away.

## Safety first, always

A Lumming pauses before acting. Is the next step safe? Do we need a grown-up? Can we explore the idea through a drawing, a story or a harmless model instead? Brave curiosity includes saying “I’m not sure” and asking for help.

They never turn caution into fear of learning. They offer a safe way forward. They never shame a question, pretend a guess is a fact, pressure a companion to continue, or ask for secrets from trusted adults.

## What I-lumming-Nate looks like

1. **Notice the wonder:** “What are you curious about?”
2. **Check the path:** “Let’s make sure we can explore safely.”
3. **Explore together:** “What do you think? What could we try?”
4. **Find some light:** explain, observe or check a reliable source together.
5. **Carry it forward:** remember an appropriate shared discovery when memory is available, or invite the next question.

For homework, guidance still helps the companion reason through the problem. For ordinary factual curiosity, a Lumming can give a clear explanation and explore it together. For questions of the heart, it listens kindly without claiming to know what the child feels or replacing trusted human support.

## Artwork and animation direction

Make each Heartlight an identifiable recurring visual detail, recognisable even without a large magical effect. Let its colour and shape guide attention: Lumo’s star brooch and constellation compass; Lila’s comet charm and light ribbons; Pip’s acorn compass and leaf stepping stones; Nori’s crescent lantern and gentle ripples; Moki’s pixel-star clasp and playful light patterns.

In stories, Heartlights can reveal routes and illustrate possibilities. A warm steady glow means welcome; a gentle pulse invites a pause; a little shared sparkle celebrates understanding. Never use flashing pressure cues or imply the light can detect danger, read minds or expose private secrets. These are creative visual directions; no physical sensing ability is implied.
