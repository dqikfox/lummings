# Lummings software-first launch strategy

Decision: 13 October 2026. Software is the current product; hardware is deferred until demand and sustained use justify it. This strategy supersedes hardware-first launch sequencing and earlier channel checklists. No campaign has been published by creating this document.

## Positioning and offer

Brand line: **Little friends. Big discoveries.**

Parent-facing proposition: **Meet Lumo and the Lummings: magical companions that turn everyday questions into little adventures.**

First audience hypothesis: parents of children aged 6–9 who already enjoy imaginative stories, puzzles and asking questions together. Confirm through interviews and supervised trials. Market to the adult purchaser; let characters make the experience appealing to the child.

Launch offer: a parent-led early-access software experience featuring Lumo, with Luna, Sprout, Nori and Moki introduced through the world. Do not imply that all five have equally complete interactive experiences until demonstrated. Choose supported devices and the inference architecture before promising availability, offline operation or setup simplicity.

## The first conversion journey

1. A parent sees a short, specific discovery with Lumo.
2. One landing page explains the experience, intended ages, actual supported devices, data handling, availability and cost.
3. One primary CTA: Try Lumo when a suitable working demo exists; until then Join parent early access.
4. Demo visitors experience a short authored quest without supplying a child's name or personal story. Clearly distinguish an authored preview from live generated conversation.
5. Adults can separately opt into updates or request supervised beta access. Do not require marketing consent to use the demo.
6. A welcome message explains what works now and invites one useful next step.
7. Pilot families receive a fresh adventure and a simple feedback prompt, without pressure or streak penalties.

Avoid competing shop, hardware preorder, asset-pack and unrelated-project CTAs in this journey. Developer downloads can remain separately accessible.

## Campaign concept: What will your light discover?

Show the Heartlight doing something meaningful: revealing a clue, marking a thoughtful pause, celebrating a discovery. Use Lumo's ruby-red light and mystical gamer charisma as the recurring visual anchor.

Repeatable content formats:

| Format | Example hook | Proof or payoff | CTA |
|---|---|---|---|
| One question, one discovery | Why does the moon seem to follow the car? | A brief accurate explanation and an invitation to notice something | Explore with Lumo |
| Meet your crew | Meet Luna. Even shadows have stories. | A distinctive character moment; label noninteractive character previews | Meet the Lummings |
| Co-op quest | Lumo found a locked constellation. Can you spot the pattern? | A solvable visual puzzle, with a friendly hint | Try the next quest |
| Parent walkthrough | Here is exactly what a first Lumo adventure looks like | Actual setup and interaction footage | Join parent early access |
| Build with us | We changed this after parents tried it | A concrete improvement and honest limitation | Help shape the beta |

Produce three short clips per week from one recording session as an initial sustainable cadence, not a platform requirement. Put the interesting question or moment first. Use readable captions and a consistent character/light treatment. Prefer actual software footage for capability claims; label concept animation. No fabricated family reactions or testimonials.

## Channel priorities

1. Parent and family-learning communities: participate where permitted, seek moderator permission for recruitment where needed, and offer a useful sample adventure. Prioritise thoughtful replies over cross-post volume.
2. Small parent, storytelling and family-learning creators: identify audience fit and arrange a few honest trials. Prepare outreach but send only when authorised. Do not condition gifts, access or compensation on praise. Clearly disclose material connections where applicable.
3. One principal short-video channel selected from actual parent interview habits. Repurpose to another only when effort stays small. Platform popularity alone does not establish buyer fit.
4. An adult opt-in email list: a portable relationship for pilot invitations and new adventures. Keep launch, feedback and unsubscribe expectations clear.
5. Developer communities: secondary for contributors and technical feedback. Their interest is not evidence of family demand.

Search content comes later, built from actual recurring parent questions. Paid acquisition waits until a working offer, attributable adult conversion and return behaviour exist. No assumed budget; if paid tests are approved, use a capped experiment with an explicit stop rule.

## Four-week execution board

| Week | Deliverables | Evidence sought |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Interview 5–8 parents; define supported platform; one offer; audit existing public claims; record one genuine interaction | Do parents understand the promise and identify a use occasion? |
| 2 | Landing-page draft; a short authored/demo quest; 3 clips; adult early-access flow; recruit 10–15 pilot families through permitted channels | Where does interest become activation or drop off? |
| 3 | Begin supervised pilot; release another quest; interview families; test two message variants sequentially if traffic is small | Which promise attracts suitable families, and why do they return? |
| 4 | Publish an honest progress/demo update; fix largest onboarding issue; test a real paid offer only if billing and delivery are ready | Is there voluntary repeat use and actual purchase intent or purchases? |

Continue the pilot long enough to observe four weeks of use; the campaign calendar does not compress that evidence into two weeks. A public launch date follows readiness, not this calendar.

## Measurement and decisions

Use adult-source tags and minimal consented aggregate counts. Do not silently add child tracking or raw conversation collection. First audit the repository's no-analytics claims and explain any actual measurement before enabling it. Manual adult surveys are acceptable for early validation.

- Acquisition: qualified adult visits by source and message; distinguish friends, developers and target parents.
- Conversion: adult early-access requests / qualified visits; demo starts and completed first adventures only where measured transparently.
- Activation: pilot families completing setup and one useful adventure / enrolled families.
- Return: activated families reporting voluntary use in week 2 and week 4 / activated families, with sample counts and missing responses shown.
- Value: parent-described benefit, favourite repeated activity and willingness to recommend for a specific reason.
- Economics: purchases, refunds, support time and actual inference/hosting cost per active family. Email signup is not a purchase.

Set pilot acceptance targets before recruitment. Use counts and interviews for small samples rather than declaring an A/B winner from a handful of clicks. High clicks with poor activation means the promise/setup needs work. Good activation with poor return means the experience needs work. Strong use with no payment means revisit the buyer, offer or price.

## Pricing hypothesis

Separate the free sample from the sustainable paid product. Test a straightforward paid starter adventure collection first if it delivers standalone value; a recurring plan is an alternative only if continued content/service value and running costs justify it. Neither pricing nor subscription policy is final. Never charge to preserve a child's friendship or memories. Put purchase, cancellation and refunds in the adult flow. Audit older free/no-subscription promises before changing the actual offer.

## Wider-launch gate

Proceed when supported devices and setup work, families demonstrate voluntary return, parents understand data handling, critical safety issues are resolved, a deliverable offer and support/refund flow exist, and running costs have been measured. Increase reach in stages; retain the full crew as a creative world while extending proven interaction quality.

## Sources and status

Channel priorities, cadence, age band and pilot sizes are recommendations to test, not market findings. Existing repository campaign files were reviewed and include outdated cast, hardware and offline claims. No live-site claim audit, outreach, publication, analytics installation or payment setup has occurred in this task.
